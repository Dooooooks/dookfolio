I have always liked to think of myself as someone who can spot a scam instantly. Working with computers every day gives you a certain kind of confidence. You write code, you see how systems fail, and you believe you are naturally guarded against bad actors.

Today almost proved me completely wrong.

An email landed in my inbox offering me a job. Lately, I have been feeling the pressure and looking hard for opportunities, so my guard was not where it usually is. That bit of eagerness made me vulnerable. For a minute, I wanted the offer to be real so badly that my critical thinking slipped, and I came dangerously close to buying into it.

Before taking the bait, something felt just slightly off. I paused, stepped back from the keyboard, and looked into the sender and the company. A quick search was all it took to reveal that the whole thing was fake.

It was a sharp wake-up call for me. Social engineering does not rely on bugs in our software; it targets moments when we are tired, hopeful, or stressed. No matter how much experience you have, stay grounded, trust your instincts, and always take the time to double-check.
